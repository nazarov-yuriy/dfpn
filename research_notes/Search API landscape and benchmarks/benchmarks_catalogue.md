# Benchmarks used to evaluate web-search APIs and search-augmented agents (state as of 2026-11-05)

Attribution convention used throughout: **[VENDOR]** = number published by a company that sells one of the compared products (or by a model lab grading its own model); **[INDEP]** = third-party evaluator or academic group with no product in the comparison; **[ACADEMIC]** = benchmark paper authors reporting baselines. Dates are the run/publication dates stated by the source; where a source gives no date, that is noted.

## Key Question 1: Full catalogue of benchmarks, with specifics

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 the search-API market is scored on roughly four families: (a) short-answer factual recall (SimpleQA, SimpleQA Verified, AA-Omniscience, FreshQA), (b) multi-hop "needle" browsing (BrowseComp and its -Plus/-ZH/Live variants, FRAMES, WebWalkerQA, xbench-DeepSearch, SealQA, GAIA, HLE-with-tools), (c) exhaustive/long-form synthesis (DeepSearchQA, WideSearch, DeepWideSearch, Mind2Web 2, LiveResearchBench, DeepWeb-Bench, TREC RAG), and (d) classical retrieval-only IR (BEIR/MTEB, MS MARCO Web Search, TREC iKAT). The only widely cited third-party composite for search APIs is the Artificial Analysis Search Index (launched Aug 2026); everything else is either academic, vendor-run, or small independent boards (OpenBenchmarks, AIMultiple).

### Cited Findings

#### SimpleQA (OpenAI, Oct/Nov 2024)
- 4,326 short fact-seeking questions; each must have a single indisputable answer, be time-invariant ("evergreen"), be backed by cited sources from at least two unique website domains, and be adversarial: at least one of four GPT-4/GPT-3.5 completions had to be wrong. Authors are OpenAI (Wei et al.); arXiv v1 dated 7 Nov 2024 — [SimpleQA paper, arXiv 2411.04368](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- Grader is a prompted ChatGPT classifier assigning correct / incorrect / not attempted; metrics are overall correct, correct-given-attempted, and their harmonic mean (F-score). Estimated inherent dataset error rate ~3% (third trainer scored 94.4% on 1,000 random questions; 15 of 56 flagged items were grader false negatives). — [SimpleQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- Topic distribution: Science & tech 19.8%, Politics 16.4%, Art 12.7%, Other 11.0%, Geography 9.8%, Sports 8.5%, Music 7.9%, TV 6.8%, History 4.0%, Video games 3.1%. Answer types: dates 32.8%, person 24.1%, number 15.3%, place 9.9%, other 18.0%. — [SimpleQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- **Source composition: "Wikipedia is by far the most common source, cited for about 3.5k of the 4.3k questions"**; next are fandom.com (410 questions), ac.uk (154), imdb.com (121). — [SimpleQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- The F-score "can reward guessing when a model is at least 50% confident"; the paper does not discuss search-tool settings and frames the benchmark as a test of whether models "know what they know." — [SimpleQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- Vendor use: Tavily runs the full set, feeds retrieved docs to gpt-4.1 to extract an answer, grades with the official classifier; reports Tavily 93.3%, Perplexity Search 85.92%, Google-via-Serper 82.15%, Brave 76.05%, Exa 71.24% (undated, [VENDOR]). — [tavily-search-evals README](https://github.com/tavily-ai/tavily-search-evals)
- Perplexity (25 Sep 2025, [VENDOR]) single-step harness: SimpleQA Perplexity .930, Exa .781, Brave .822, "SERP based" (Tavily) .890. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- Brave (5 Aug 2025, updated 1 Sep 2025, [VENDOR]): AI Grounding single search F1 92.1%; research-with-reasoning multi-search F1 94.1% (Acc 93.25%). Cites Perplexity Sonar Pro F1 85.5%, Perplexity Deep Research 93.9%, Tavily+GPT-4.1 Acc* 93.3%, Exa Research Pro Acc* 94.9%. Brave's human review of the 292 judge-marked-wrong answers found 167 judge errors. F1 varies up to 0.2% run-to-run. — [Brave AI Grounding blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/)
- You.com (26 Aug 2025, [VENDOR]): 77.84% (95% CI 76.60–79.08), p50 445 ms; GPT-4o-mini answering agent, GPT-4.1 grader with OpenAI's official prompt. — [You.com report](https://about.you.com/resources/search-api-for-the-agentic-era)
- Firecrawl (July 2026, [VENDOR]): GPT-5.4 agent, up to 20 tool calls, Firecrawl Search + Scrape, full question set, official grader; reported the better of two sessions: 94.7% with search vs 43.8% without. — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark)
- Keiro Labs (undated, © 2026, [VENDOR]): measures only answer *presence* in retrieved text on 500 random questions: 92.0% with full crawl (~37,000 tokens read), 59.4% from titles+snippets only, 75.6% when trimmed to ~900 BM25-selected tokens; they add Wikipedia's own search "because SimpleQA leans on Wikipedia"; suggest ±3-point run variance. — [Keiro lite-search-benchmark](https://github.com/Keirolabs-API/lite-search-benchmark)
- Linkup (Feb 2025 blog, [VENDOR]): 90.10% Deep Search, 91.0% in March 2025 update; competitor numbers were taken from competitors' own publications, not re-run. — [Linkup blog](https://www.linkup.so/blog/linkup-establishes-sota-performance-on-simpleqa). Current benchmarks page: Linkup Fast 94%, Perplexity Sonar 87%, Gemini 3 Flash 85%, Serper Fast 83%, Tavily Fast 79%, Exa Fast 63%; answering LLM, result count and judge not stated. — [Linkup benchmarks](https://www.linkup.so/benchmarks)
- Saturation: OpenAI described SimpleQA as "already saturated" by models with fast browsing tools (April 2025, as quoted by Firecrawl). — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark)

#### SimpleQA Verified (Google DeepMind / Google Research, 9 Sep 2025)
- 1,000-question cleaned subset of SimpleQA; authors Haas, D'Antonio, Das (DeepMind), Yona, Goldshtein (Google Research). Problems identified in the original: narrow source distribution attributed to "substantial human rater biases", incorrect ground truths (no % given), topic skew ("over-indexes on science and technology"), answer-type skew (32.8% dates, 24.1% names), redundancy (119 questions = 2.7% ask about founding dates of Colombian municipalities). — [SimpleQA Verified, arXiv 2509.07968](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Filtering pipeline: 4,326 → 3,095 unique source docs (−28.5%) → 2,871 after semantic dedup (Gemini Embeddings, 0.77 cosine) → 2,664 after TF-IDF dedup → 1,855 after removing publishers opting out of Google/OpenAI/Anthropic crawling → 1,218 after topic/answer-type balancing → 1,117/1,073 after source reconciliation → 1,000 hardest kept for headroom. Metadata: 3.7% reasoning, 7.3% multi-step. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Autorater gpt-4.1-2025-04-14 with a prompt 15% longer (963→1,124 words): explicit numeric tolerance ranges, hedging must commit, punting examples added. Top no-tool F1: Gemini 2.5 Pro 55.6, GPT-5 52.3, o3 51.9. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Explicit limitation: **"Enabling tools on SimpleQA Verified results in near perfect performance"**; the benchmark is meant to be run without tools. Several authors are at Google and Gemini 2.5 Pro (the top scorer) was also used for embeddings/filtering. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1); quoted in [Parallel SimpleQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/simpleqa-benchmark.md)
- Vendor use (Parallel, run 9 Sep 2026, 100-question sample, [VENDOR]): frontier tier (GPT-5.6 Sol, high) Parallel Advanced 97%, Parallel Basic 97%, Perplexity 95%, Tavily 92%, Exa Auto 91%; low-cost tier (GPT-5.6 Luna, low) Parallel Fast 94%, Perplexity 94%, Tavily 94%, Parallel Turbo 91%, Exa Auto 91%. All ten configurations fall in 91–97%. — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis); [Parallel SimpleQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/simpleqa-benchmark.md)
- SerpApi (posted 19 Oct, year not shown; models imply 2026; [VENDOR]): 97.6% (937/960) with GPT-6.1 Sol (low) agent, SerpApi Google + Extract, grader GPT-5.6 Sol (high); **excluded 40 questions "where search results exposed benchmark answers or evaluation hints"** (≈4% of the set). Competitor numbers (Tavily 97.6%, Parallel 97.0%, Perplexity 95%, Exa 91%) were copied from vendor reports, not re-run. — [SerpApi dev.to post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5)

#### BrowseComp (OpenAI, 16 Apr 2025)
- 1,266 questions requiring "persistent internet navigation" for "hard-to-find, entangled information" with short, easily verified answers. Trainers confirmed GPT-4o (with/without browsing), o1 and an early Deep Research could not solve them and ran five Google searches to check the answer was not on the first result pages; tasks a second trainer solved >40% of the time were sent back. — [BrowseComp paper, arXiv 2504.12516](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516v1); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12516)
- Human baseline (2-hour limit): 367/1,255 solved (29.2%), 888 gave up (70.8%); 86.4% of solved answers matched the reference. Model scores: GPT-4o 0.6% (calibration error 69%), GPT-4o w/ browsing 1.9% (82%), GPT-4.5 0.9%, o1 9.9%, Deep Research 51.5% (calibration error 91%). Over 64 trials Deep Research solved 16% of tasks every time and 14% never. Grader uses the same prompt as Humanity's Last Exam. Best-of-N over 64 samples adds ~15–25%. — [BrowseComp paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516v1)
- Abstract-level limitations: sidesteps "a true user query distribution", does not test long-form generation or ambiguity resolution. — [BrowseComp abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12516)
- Parallel's compilation notes OpenAI removed 21 questions with bad labels; standard error ≈6.5 points at 50 questions, 4.6 at 100, 3.2 at 200; "questions still leak" (a search for the trainer exemplar returned pages quoting it verbatim); top deep-research systems in the 90s on a 100-question subset so "headroom at the top is shrinking". — [Parallel BrowseComp explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark)
- Search-API scores on BrowseComp: AA (19 Oct 2026, 200-q hard subset, [INDEP]) Perplexity medium 87, Perplexity high 86, Octen 86, Exa auto 83, Exa lite 81, Parallel advanced 77, Brave 77, You.com 77, Nimble 75, OpenAI Web Search 74, Firecrawl 74, Parallel basic 73, no-search 17. — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api); [Parallel compilation](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark)
- Parallel (9 Sep 2026, 50-q sample, [VENDOR]): frontier Parallel Advanced 74%, Perplexity 74%, Parallel Basic 72%, Exa Auto 70%, Tavily 66%; low-cost Perplexity 46%, Parallel Fast 44%, Exa Auto 36%, Parallel Turbo 32%, Tavily 32%. Cost per 1k questions at frontier: Perplexity $275, Parallel Advanced $399, Tavily $935, Exa $971. — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- Parallel earlier run (10–12 Jul 2026, [VENDOR]): Parallel Turbo 51%, Brave 38.3%, Exa Instant 33.7%, Tavily Ultra Fast 19.3%. — [Parallel "best AI search for agents"](https://parallel.ai/articles/best-ai-search-for-agents)
- Perplexity (Sep 2025, [VENDOR], deep-research harness): Perplexity .371, Exa .265, Brave .221, Tavily .348. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- Deep-research-product scores (Parallel Task API eval, 26 Aug 2026, 100-q fixed subset, [VENDOR]): Parallel Ultra4x 94%, Ultra2x 93%, Ultra 92%, Core 91%, Lite 88%; Perplexity (high) 86%; GPT-5.6 Sol PTC (max) 85%; Exa Agent Max 78%; Gemini 3.1 Pro (high) 72%. — [Parallel compilation](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark). Perplexity's own agent-API harness (2026, [VENDOR]): Perplexity 0.805, OpenAI 0.720, Anthropic 0.598, Parallel 0.56, Exa 0.38. — [perplexityai/search_evals](https://github.com/perplexityai/search_evals)
- Open-model agent scores: MiroThinker-1.7 reports 74.0 BrowseComp / 75.3 BrowseComp-ZH ([VENDOR]). — [MiroThinker GitHub](https://github.com/MiroMindAI/MiroThinker)

#### BrowseComp-Plus (Univ. of Waterloo + CMU/CSIRO/UQ, 8 Aug 2025) — fixed corpus
- Motivation: live search APIs are "dynamic and opaque", hurting fairness/reproducibility, and make it impossible to separate retriever contribution from agent reasoning; commercial APIs also add cost. — [BrowseComp-Plus, arXiv 2508.06600](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)
- Construction: from 1,266 BrowseComp questions, o3-with-search cited evidence URLs; pages scraped (Selenium + Trafilatura); 137 unscrapable pairs dropped (1,005 left); 14 student annotators (~400 hours, >80% agreement) verified gold documents; 830 queries passed. Hard negatives: GPT-4o decomposed each query into ~7 sub-queries sent to Google via SerpAPI (up to 100 results each). Final corpus 100,195 documents; per query 6.1 evidence docs, 76.28 negatives, 2.9 gold docs. Excluded 42 Google-Maps-dependent and 13 ambiguous queries. — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)
- Metrics: accuracy (judge gpt-4.1), evidence recall, search calls per query, calibration error, plus Recall@k / nDCG@k with TREC-style qrels. Retriever returns top-5, 512-token truncation (86.5% of queries still contain the answer in a truncated gold doc). — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)
- Results (accuracy, BM25 → Qwen3-Embedding-8B): gpt-5 55.90 → 70.12; o3 49.28 → 63.49; gpt-oss-120B-high 28.67 → 42.89; Claude Sonnet 4 14.34 → 36.75; Qwen3-32B 3.49 → 10.36; Search-R1-32B 3.86 → 10.36. Oracle retrieval: gpt-4.1 93.49%, Qwen3-32B 83.25%. Better retrieval cut search calls by ~1–3 per query for proprietary models. 10× corpus expansion (FineWeb-Edu, 9.77M docs) lowered recall; Qwen3-32B fell 10.36 → 7.11. — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)
- Limitations: static corpus far smaller than the web; hard negatives from one provider (Google/SerpAPI); single LLM judge; evidence partly derived from o3 output. — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)

#### BrowseComp-ZH (Apr 2025)
- 289 multi-hop questions, 11 domains, Chinese web; reverse-engineered from short verifiable answers; two-stage quality control; >20 systems tested, many <10%, few >20%; best OpenAI DeepResearch 42.9%. — [BrowseComp-ZH, arXiv 2504.19314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19314)

#### FRAMES (Google, Sep 2024; NAACL 2025)
- 824 multi-hop questions requiring 2–15 Wikipedia articles; ~36% multi-constraint reasoning, 20% numerical comparison, 16% temporal disambiguation. Scoring = accuracy (response contains gold answer). Baselines: no retrieval 0.40/0.408, BM25 top-4 0.474, multi-step iterative retrieval 0.66, oracle ~0.73. — [FRAMES abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12941); [alphaXiv overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2409.12941v3); [Parallel FRAMES explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/frames-benchmark)
- Vendor use: Perplexity (Sep 2025, [VENDOR]) FRAMES Perplexity .453, Tavily .437, Exa .399, Brave .320. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api). Exa docs list FRAMES, MultiLoKo and BrowseComp as targets for its "Deep" mode. — [Exa docs](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/evaluating-exa-search). You.com's eval guide lists FRAMES for "multi-step reasoning". — [You.com evaluate-us](https://you.com/docs/evaluate-us.md)

#### DeepSearchQA (Google DeepMind / Search / Kaggle, released Dec 2025; paper 28 Jan 2026)
- 900 prompts, 17 fields; multi-step "causal chain" questions whose answers are single values or exhaustive sets; outcome-only grading; primary metric F1 over answer items (plus Fully Correct / Partially Correct / Correct-with-Extraneous / Fully Incorrect); judge Gemini 2.5 Flash zero-shot; three-reviewer verification; Kaggle ran leaderboard evaluations. — [DeepSearchQA, arXiv 2601.20975](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20975v1)
- Paper results (F1 / Fully Correct %): Gemini Deep Research Agent 81.90 / 66.09; GPT-5 Pro 78.98 / 65.18; Gemini 3 Pro Preview 76.86 / 56.56; GPT-5 73.24 / 59.41; o3 Deep Research 66.45 / 44.24; Claude 4.5 Opus 40.20 / 24.01; Claude 4.5 Haiku 22.24 / 12.78. "Last Mile Problem" = ~15-point gap between F1 and Fully Correct. n=8 sampling lifts Fully Correct to 85.71%. — [DeepSearchQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20975v1)
- Kaggle leaderboard as of 10 Dec 2025: Gemini DR Agent 66.1%, GPT-5 Pro 65.2%, GPT-5 59.4%, o3 DR 44.2%, Claude Opus 4.5 24.0% (Fully Incorrect 50.7%). — [implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/google-turns-research-agents-into-infrastructure-and-writes-the-test-theyre-graded-on)
- Prompts are time-anchored/static-source to limit drift; no explicit contamination controls described; stale ground truth acknowledged. — [DeepSearchQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20975v1)
- Use for search APIs: it is one-third of the AA Search Index (full 900-task eval split, LLM-graded F1). AA 19 Oct 2026 DSQA F1 [INDEP]: Exa auto 86, Exa lite 85, Parallel advanced 81, Perplexity medium 81, Octen 80, Brave LLM-context 78, OpenAI Web Search 78, You.com 77, Nimble 76, DeepSeek 75, Firecrawl 74, no-search 45. — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api). Perplexity's agent-API harness ([VENDOR]) DSQA: Perplexity 0.871, Anthropic 0.815, Parallel 0.81, OpenAI 0.733, Exa 0.53. — [search_evals](https://github.com/perplexityai/search_evals). Parallel's Task API eval uses a fixed 100-question subset (26 Aug 2026). — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- Also available in EvalScope and Harbor harnesses. — [EvalScope docs](https://evalscope.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks/deepsearchqa.html)

#### Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) in search settings
- HLE was built to resist web lookup (creators tried to keep questions out of search and training data). — [VKTR explainer](https://www.vktr.com/ai-technology/humanitys-last-exam/)
- OpenAI Deep Research scored 26.6% (Feb 2025) with web search enabled — roughly 3× the best tool-free score at the time; press noted search access makes comparison to tool-free models unfair. — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/02/12/openai-deepresearch-humanity-last-exam); [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250205-openai-deep-research-high-score)
- Perplexity Deep Research 21.1% (14 Feb 2025); community "agents with tools" leaderboard lists Gemini Deep Research 46.4 (11 Dec 2025) — unverified against primary source. — [HLE agents-with-tools leaderboard](https://zoom-ai-hle-leaderboard.hf.space/)
- Perplexity's search-API harness uses an HLE text-only retrieval subset of 2,158 questions; Sep 2025 scores [VENDOR]: Perplexity .288, Tavily .248, Exa .242, Brave .191; 2026 agent-API scores: OpenAI 0.614, Perplexity 0.612, Anthropic 0.566, Parallel 0.515, Exa 0.387. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api); [search_evals](https://github.com/perplexityai/search_evals)
- No-tool HLE (AA leaderboard, independent): Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview 44.7%, GPT-5.4 41.6% (date of snapshot unclear). — [AA HLE page](https://www.artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/humanitys-last-exam)

#### GAIA (Meta/HF, Nov 2023)
- 466 questions, 300 with private answers for the leaderboard; tests reasoning, multimodality, web browsing, tool use; humans 92% vs GPT-4+plugins 15%. — [GAIA abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983). Three difficulty levels; many evaluators use the 103-question text-only validation subset, others the 165/166-question full validation set — scores are not comparable across subsets. — [search summary of GAIA usage](https://forkast.news/glossary/gaia-benchmark/); [Tongyi DeepResearch report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24701)
- Independent academic evidence that the search backend matters on GAIA (Jun 2025, [ACADEMIC]): same agent resolved 56.96% with Brave, 58.18% with Exa, 64.24% with Tavily. — [arXiv 2506.03011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03011)
- H2O.ai reported 75% on GAIA (Mar 2025, [VENDOR]) and flagged contamination as a concern. — [H2O blog](https://h2o.ai/blog/2025/h2o-ai-tops-the-general-ai-assistant-test/)

#### WebWalkerQA (Alibaba Tongyi, Jan 2025)
- 680 queries over 1,373 webpages, four real-world scenarios, bilingual EN/ZH; measures systematic traversal of a website's subpages ("vertical" navigation) rather than horizontal search. — [WebWalkerQA, arXiv 2501.07572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07572v3); [Tongyi DeepResearch report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24701)

#### SealQA (Virginia Tech group; Jun 2025, ICLR 2026)
- Fact-seeking questions where web search returns conflicting, noisy or unhelpful results; subsets Seal-0 (main; 111 questions per SerpApi's 65/111 report), Seal-Hard, LongSeal. o3 17.1% and o4-mini 6.3% at best reasoning effort with tools; chat models like GPT-4.1 near zero; increasing test-time compute does not reliably help. — [SealQA abstract, arXiv 2506.01062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01062); [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5)
- Human testers with Google averaged 38.8% / 64.0% (avg / best) on 50 Seal-Hard questions; GPT-4.1-mini dropped 13.8% → 11.8% after enabling built-in search. — [Parallel SealQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/sealqa-benchmark.md)
- Vendor use [VENDOR]: Linkup Research 61%, Parallel Ultra 8X 56%, GPT-5 48%, Exa Research Pro 45%, Perplexity DR 38%, Tavily Research 24% on Seal-0 (undated). — [Linkup benchmarks](https://www.linkup.so/benchmarks); SerpApi 58.56% on Seal-0. — [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5)

#### FreshQA (Google/UMass/OpenAI, Oct 2023)
- 600 questions in four categories (never-changing, slow-changing, fast-changing, false-premise); two-mode (RELAXED/STRICT) evaluation based on >50K human judgments; authors "commit to updating it at regular intervals"; FreshPrompt adds search results to the prompt. — [FreshLLMs abstract, arXiv 2310.03214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03214); [DAIR summary](https://academy.dair.ai/papers/freshllms-freshqa)
- Vendor use [VENDOR]: Valyu reports 79% vs Parallel 52%, Google 39%, Exa 24% (Aug 27 test set, run 3 Oct 2025). — [Valyu blog](https://www.valyu.ai/blogs/benchmarking-search-apis-for-ai-agents). You.com lists FreshQA with its `freshness` parameter. — [You.com evaluate-us](https://you.com/docs/evaluate-us.md)

#### WideSearch (Aug 2025)
- 200 manually curated tasks (100 EN, 100 ZH, 15+ domains) requiring collection of large-scale atomic information into an organized output; five-stage QC; "most systems achieve overall success rates near 0%, with the best performer reaching just 5%"; humans near 100% given time. Affiliation not confirmed from the abstract page. — [WideSearch, arXiv 2508.07999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07999)
- Vendor use: Parallel samples 100 tasks and scores with partial credit (Sep 2026, [VENDOR]: Parallel Advanced 57.6, Exa Auto 55.9, Tavily 55.9, Parallel Basic 55.3, Perplexity 53.5 at frontier). — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis). Perplexity's harness reports `f1_by_row` (Perplexity 0.651, Anthropic 0.590, Parallel 0.584, OpenAI 0.522, Exa 0.471, [VENDOR]). — [search_evals](https://github.com/perplexityai/search_evals)

#### DeepWideSearch (Oct 2025)
- 220 questions, 15 domains, combining multi-hop depth with large-scale width; SOTA agents 2.39% average success; failure modes: lack of reflection, over-reliance on internal knowledge, insufficient retrieval, context overflow. — [DeepWideSearch, arXiv 2510.20168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20168)

#### xbench-DeepSearch (HSG / Sequoia China; "evergreen")
- Chinese-internet oriented, human-written and cross-validated, 100 questions per release, data encrypted to prevent crawling; default judge gemini-2.0-flash; results reported in accuracy bands. 2505 release (May 2025): o3 search 65+, o4-mini-high 60+. 2510 release (evaluated Aug 2025): ChatGPT-5-Pro 75+ (~$0.085/task, 5–8 min), SuperGrok Expert 40+, a tie group at 35+ (Fellou, Minimax Agent, StepFun, Flowith, Skywork, Manus, Doubao DR). — [xbench-evals GitHub](https://github.com/xbench-ai/xbench-evals); [search summary](https://miromindai.github.io/MiroFlow/xbench_ds)

#### Mind2Web 2 (Ohio State, NeurIPS 2025 D&B)
- 130 long-horizon live-browsing tasks, 6 domains / 24 sub-domains, >1,000 human hours; Agent-as-a-Judge with tree-structured rubrics scoring correctness and source attribution; ten systems + humans; OpenAI Deep Research reached ~50–70% of human performance in about half the time. — [Mind2Web 2, arXiv 2506.21506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21506v2); [project page](https://osu-nlp-group.github.io/Mind2Web-2); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121798)

#### LiveSearchBench (Nov 2025; Shanghai AI Lab per alphaXiv)

## Key Question 2: Which benchmarks are agent-in-the-loop vs retrieval-only, and why it matters

### Takeaway
Agent-in-the-loop evaluations (AA Search Index, Parallel, OpenBenchmarks hard-retrieval/multi-hop, Firecrawl, SerpApi, Brave multi-search, Perplexity deep-research harness) let the model issue multiple queries and fetch pages, so they measure the provider's contribution to an end-to-end task and capture downstream token cost; retrieval-only setups (Tavily's repo, Perplexity single-step, You.com 2025, Keiro, Exa pointwise grading, AIMultiple, OpenBenchmarks factual-lookup, BEIR/MTEB) measure what one call returns. The two can rank the same providers differently, and the choice of which tools the agent gets (extract/fetch or not) changes winners.

### Cited Findings
- **Agent-in-the-loop designs:** AA gives the model `web_search` + `web_fetch` + `finish` with 25 turns and unlimited tool calls; the model "decides when to search, reads sources, and submits an answer." — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/search-api); [AA launch](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/search-api). Parallel's harness is an "agentic, multi-step loop, not a single top-k reader", with each provider's search tool plus extract tool where one exists (Parallel, Exa, Tavily); Perplexity was search-only. — [Parallel SimpleQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/simpleqa-benchmark.md). Firecrawl: GPT-5.4 agent, ≤20 tool calls, search + scrape. — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark). SerpApi: multiple searches/fetches per question, 48k chars per fetch, 56k tokens input. — [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5). Brave's "research with reasoning, multiple searches" averaged 7 searches and 210 unique pages per question. — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/). OpenBenchmarks hard-retrieval (5 searches/32 turns) and multi-hop (8 turns/14 searches). — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search)
- **Retrieval-only / single-call designs:** Tavily's repo runs each provider once and hands the documents to gpt-4.1 to extract an answer. — [tavily-search-evals](https://github.com/tavily-ai/tavily-search-evals). Perplexity's 2025 "single-step search" harness for SimpleQA and FRAMES vs a separate deep-research harness for BrowseComp and HLE. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api). You.com's 2025 report: an LLM agent answers from each provider's results, grader GPT-4.1; its guide measures p50 around the search call only. — [You.com report](https://about.you.com/resources/search-api-for-the-agentic-era); [You.com guide](https://you.com/docs/evaluate-us.md). Keiro measures answer presence only. — [Keiro](https://github.com/Keirolabs-API/lite-search-benchmark). Exa's "pure result grading" assigns an LLM relevance score per result (pointwise). — [Exa evals](https://exa.ai/blog/evals-at-exa). AIMultiple judges top-5 results per query, not answers. — [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search). OpenBenchmarks factual lookup: one request, reader writes from titles/snippets; also reports AR@1/AR@5 (answer already in snippets). — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search). BEIR/MTEB/MS MARCO Web Search are pure ranking metrics (nDCG, Recall@k, MRR). — [MS MARCO Web Search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.07526); [tutorial](https://pendu.github.io/Advanced-Retreival-and-Re-ranking/benchmarks_and_datasets.html)
- **Fixed-corpus hybrid:** BrowseComp-Plus keeps the agent loop but swaps the live API for a 100k-doc corpus with retrievers (BM25, Qwen3-Embedding, ReasonIR) so the retriever's contribution is isolated; stronger retrieval raised GPT-5 from 55.9% to 70.1% and reduced search calls. — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1)
- **Why it matters — winners change with mode:** OpenBenchmarks: "the top provider changes with tool setup, for example Exa deep with fetch versus Perplexity with snippets only" (multi-hop F1 Exa deep 48.2 with fetch vs Parallel basic 46.5 search-only). — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search). Parallel notes Brave scored 75 on AA level with Parallel advanced but Brave "ran without the web_fetch tool that engines like Parallel, Exa, and Tavily were given." — [Parallel Brave comparison](https://parallel.ai/articles/brave-search-api-vs-parallel.md). TinyFish observes that AA's shared text-only `web_fetch` means "native scraping, browser automation, and deep-research products are not scored." — [TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/search-api-benchmark)
- **Why it matters — cost shifts to the model:** AA: "Model cost varies by provider because returned payloads, search counts, and reasoning tokens differ"; Parallel advanced costs more per search than basic but less in total ($0.084 vs $0.11 per task) because focused results use fewer model tokens; Exa fast costs more per task than Exa auto despite being faster per query because it runs more searches. — [AA launch](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/search-api). Firecrawl's excerpt mode uses "about 10x fewer tokens than full pages." — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark)
- **Why it matters — the agent can mask the search:** LiveBrowseComp shows more than half of agent queries originate from the model's own hypotheses and retrieved evidence is used less than a third of the time; blocking evidence drops avg pass@4 from 26.1 to 6.2, meaning static-benchmark agent scores partly reflect parametric knowledge, not retrieval. — [LiveBrowseComp](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.28721). DeepWeb-Bench attributes only 12–14% of frontier-model errors to retrieval. — [DeepWeb-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21482)
- **Why it matters — harness tuning:** AA finds search makes the model write 2–3× as many output tokens on Omniscience. — [AA launch](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/search-api). LiveResearchBench: "each system was configured to maximize performance, so comparisons may reflect tuning effort as well as capability." — [LiveResearchBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.14240)
- Exa's docs recommend using identical query sets, the same LLM for synthesis and grading, disabling prompt engineering, and comparing only against the closest competitor by P50 latency. — [Exa docs](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/evaluating-exa-search)
- Independent academic datapoint that backend choice moves agent scores: same GAIA agent scored 56.96% (Brave), 58.18% (Exa), 64.24% (Tavily). — [arXiv 2506.03011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03011)

### Inferences
- Agent-in-the-loop numbers are a joint property of (model, prompt, tool set, turn budget, provider); only AA and OpenBenchmarks hold everything but the provider fixed and publish it, so they are the only cross-vendor numbers that are even nominally comparable. Vendor tables that mix harnesses (Linkup 2025, SerpApi, Brave's competitor rows) are not comparable even when they cite the same benchmark.
- Retrieval-only metrics reward snippet quality and index coverage; agent-loop metrics additionally reward payload shape (highlights vs full page) and latency per call because they influence how many turns the agent spends. Providers that win on one (e.g., Brave on AIMultiple relevance; Exa on OpenBenchmarks hard retrieval with fetch) need not win on the other.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much of the AA Index rank order would change if `web_fetch` were disabled or a different candidate model were used; AA has not published a sensitivity analysis.
- No independent study was found that runs the *same* providers through both a retrieval-only and an agent-loop harness and reports rank correlation.

## Key Question 3: SimpleQA's composition and the Wikipedia/top-k bias

### Takeaway
By OpenAI's own count roughly 3.5k of 4.3k SimpleQA questions (~81%) cite Wikipedia as a source, and tool-enabled systems reach "near perfect" scores; vendors therefore cluster at 91–97% and small gaps are within noise or judge error. Explicit evidence that Wikipedia-weighting *inflates* search-API scores comes from Keiro (adding Wikipedia search as a free booster) and Search Arena (Wikipedia citations are disfavoured by real users), but no source measured the inflation directly.

### Cited Findings
- "Wikipedia is by far the most common source, cited for about 3.5k of the 4.3k questions"; other frequent domains fandom.com (410), ac.uk (154), imdb.com (121). Each question had to have two unique source domains. — [SimpleQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04368v1)
- SimpleQA Verified: questions "stem from a narrow distribution of source documents" due to "substantial human rater biases"; de-duplicating by source URL alone cut 4,326 → 3,095; 119 questions ask founding dates of Colombian municipalities; the set "over-indexes on science and technology." — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- "Enabling tools on SimpleQA Verified results in near perfect performance," so the benchmark "is only meaningful without tools." — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Keiro adds Wikipedia's own search API (top 3 titles) to its recipe because "SimpleQA leans on Wikipedia" and it is free; titles+snippets alone cover 59.4% of answers, full-page crawl 92.0%. — [Keiro](https://github.com/Keirolabs-API/lite-search-benchmark)
- Brave's view of inflation: pages containing benchmark Q&A "have appeared online, likely entering training data and search indexes", so retrieved answers may reflect "memorization or direct exposure rather than true reasoning or retrieval quality"; Brave excluded huggingface.co/datasets URLs, URLs containing "simpleqa" and pages with "simpleqa" in the text, and says other providers do not consistently apply such filters. — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/)
- SerpApi excluded 40/1,000 SimpleQA Verified questions (4%) because "search results exposed benchmark answers or evaluation hints." — [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5)
- Parallel: "A search query can return a page that lists the benchmark item itself, which can inflate scores"; SimpleQA has been on GitHub since 2024 and now appears in papers, blogs, leaderboards and mirrors. — [Parallel SimpleQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/simpleqa-benchmark.md)
- All ten provider configurations in Parallel's Sep 2026 run scored 91–97% on SimpleQA Verified; on a 100-question sample a three-point gap is three questions. — [Parallel SimpleQA explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/simpleqa-benchmark.md)
- Judge sensitivity: Brave's reviewers found the LLM judge wrong on 167 of 292 answers it had marked incorrect (either rejecting valid answers or relying on flawed expected answers). — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/). The Verified autorater had to be changed to handle numeric ranges, hedging and punting. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Real users dislike Wikipedia citations: Search Arena found Wikipedia-citing responses had a negative preference coefficient (β=−0.071) while community/tech/social platforms were positive. — [Search Arena paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05334v2)
- A secondary wiki states ~3,500 questions cite Wikipedia and that high search-augmented scores "most likely reflect either web search / retrieval-augmented configurations or training contamination" (low-authority source, consistent with the paper's count). — [aiwiki.ai SimpleQA](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/simpleqa)
- Vendor-independent reasons scores are high: Keiro notes limiting content to ~900 tokens drops coverage from 92.0% to 75.6%, so token budget, not ranking, dominates. — [Keiro](https://github.com/Keirolabs-API/lite-search-benchmark)

### Inferences
- Because ~4 in 5 SimpleQA answers live on Wikipedia, any provider whose top-k reliably includes the relevant Wikipedia page (or whose reader is given enough tokens of it) will score ≥90%; differences between providers at that level mostly reflect the judge, the token budget, contamination handling and sampling noise rather than index quality. This makes SimpleQA a floor test ("does the API find Wikipedia?") rather than a discriminator — consistent with Google's statement that tool-enabled performance is near perfect.
- The fact that both Brave and SerpApi had to hand-filter leaked pages implies that unfiltered SimpleQA runs (most vendor runs do not mention filtering) carry an unknown contamination bonus.

### Gaps
- No source measured the fraction of SimpleQA questions *answerable from the top-k Wikipedia result alone* for each provider, or the score delta from excluding Wikipedia from the index.
- No independent (non-vendor) SimpleQA run across providers with a shared harness was found after You.com's Aug 2025 report; AA deliberately does not use SimpleQA.

## Key Question 4: Which benchmarks are saturating and which still discriminate between search backends

### Takeaway
SimpleQA and SimpleQA Verified are saturated for any tool-enabled system (91–97% across providers; "near perfect" per Google); BrowseComp is saturating for frontier deep-research products (88–94% on Parallel's 100-q subset) but still spreads search APIs 72–87 on AA's hard subset and 32–74% in Parallel's harness; DeepSearchQA (74–86), OpenBenchmarks hard-retrieval (38–83) and multi-hop (0–48 F1), SealQA, WideSearch/DeepWideSearch (≈0–5%), and the live benchmarks remain far from ceiling.

### Cited Findings
- SimpleQA: "already saturated" by browsing-enabled models (OpenAI, Apr 2025, via Firecrawl). — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark). SimpleQA Verified tool-enabled ⇒ near perfect. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1). All configurations 91–97% (Parallel, Sep 2026). — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis). OpenBenchmarks factual lookup 87.7–99.3%. — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search)
- BrowseComp: "Top deep-research systems are in the 90s on the 100-question subset, so the headroom at the top is shrinking" (Parallel Ultra4x 94%, Perplexity high 86%, Aug 2026, [VENDOR]). — [Parallel BrowseComp explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark). But among search APIs with a fixed agent, AA's hard-200 subset spans 63 (DeepSeek) – 87 (Perplexity) with no-search at 17 (Oct 2026, [INDEP]). — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api). In Parallel's harness the low-cost tier spans 32–46% and frontier 66–74%. — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- LiveBrowseComp shows BrowseComp's remaining signal is partly parametric: closed-book pass@4 up to 44.5% on BrowseComp and 62.0% on BrowseComp-ZH; live variant <2% closed-book. — [LiveBrowseComp](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.28721)
- DeepSearchQA: AA F1 spread 74–86 among providers vs 45 no-search; top agents in the paper at F1 ~82 / Fully Correct 66%. — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api); [DeepSearchQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20975v1)
- AA-Omniscience: narrow 66–73 spread among providers (TinyFish observation), with Firecrawl leading despite a lower overall index. — [TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/search-api-benchmark); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api)
- WideSearch: most systems ~0%, best 5% (Aug 2025). — [WideSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07999). DeepWideSearch 2.39% avg success (Oct 2025). — [DeepWideSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20168)
- SealQA: o3 17.1%, o4-mini 6.3%; chat models near zero. — [SealQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01062). Vendor research tiers 24–61% ([VENDOR]). — [Linkup benchmarks](https://www.linkup.so/benchmarks)
- FRAMES (search APIs, single-step, Sep 2025, [VENDOR]): 0.32–0.45. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- HLE with search: 0.19–0.29 (2025 search APIs) to 0.39–0.61 (2026 agent APIs), [VENDOR]. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api); [search_evals](https://github.com/perplexityai/search_evals)
- BrowseComp-ZH: best 42.9%, many <10% (Apr 2025). — [BrowseComp-ZH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19314). xbench-DeepSearch 2510: ChatGPT-5-Pro 75+, most agents 30–40+. — [xbench-evals](https://github.com/xbench-ai/xbench-evals)
- OpenBenchmarks hard retrieval 38.0–83.0% and multi-hop F1 0.0–48.2 across providers (Oct 2026). — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search)
- Statistical headroom: AA publishes no CIs and TinyFish warns 80 vs 79 should not be read as a quality difference; Parallel says 50 BrowseComp questions imply ±14 points at 95%; AIMultiple's top four are statistically indistinguishable. — [TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/search-api-benchmark); [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis); [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search)

### Inferences
- The discriminating power of a benchmark for *search backends* is different from its headroom for *models*: BrowseComp is near ceiling for best-of-N deep-research products yet still separates search APIs by ~25 points under a fixed single-pass agent, because the hard-200 subset and pass@1 scoring remove the test-time-compute lever.
- Benchmarks that still discriminate share two traits: they require recall over many items (DSQA, WideSearch, OpenBenchmarks multi-hop) or post-cutoff/noisy evidence (SealQA, LiveBrowseComp, NEEDLE, company-news lookups); these are exactly where index freshness and coverage, not Wikipedia retrieval, matter.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal data was found showing how AA Index scores have moved per provider between Aug and Oct 2026 beyond the two snapshots recorded above.
- No published analysis of ceiling effects on DeepSearchQA for search APIs (the max observed is 86 F1).

## Key Question 5: How evaluators measure latency and cost alongside accuracy

### Takeaway
There is no shared convention: Perplexity and You.com report request-level percentiles (p50/p75/p90/p95; p50 only), AA reports derived model time plus measured mean search time per task and per query with cost per 1,000 tasks split into model tokens and list-price search fees, Parallel reports cost per 1,000 questions (tokens + tool calls) on a log scale with no latency, OpenBenchmarks mixes mean call latency, median task time and cost per 1,000 correct answers, and AIMultiple reports mean per-call latency and a 5-call workflow total.

### Cited Findings
- Perplexity (Sep 2025, from AWS us-east-1): p50/p75/p90/p95 — Perplexity 358/443/604/763 ms; Brave 513/637/728/808; Tavily 1,342/1,586/1,743/1,790; Exa 1,375/1,499/1,798/2,188. Sample size and request mix not stated. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- You.com: p50 measured with `time.perf_counter()` around the search call only (synthesis/grading excluded); p50 = sorted latency at index len//2; recommends comparing only within the same latency class; cost tracked as $/1,000 queries. 2025 report: p50 445 ms. — [You.com guide](https://you.com/docs/evaluate-us.md); [You.com report](https://about.you.com/resources/search-api-for-the-agentic-era)
- Artificial Analysis: Time per task = model time (output tokens ÷ canonical output speed; derived) + average measured `web_search` time; Time per Search Query = average per request; cost = candidate model tokens (cache-hit pricing for earlier turns) + provider list price; reported per 1,000 tasks; OpenAI search time is "an upper limit." No percentiles. Examples: Keenable realtime 0.34 s/query (Aug 2026), Octen 0.21 s/query (Oct 2026); Firecrawl slowest at 55–62 s per task; search $/1k tasks from $0 (TinyFish) and $8.47 (DeepSeek) to $62.30 (Perplexity medium). — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/search-api); [AA launch](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/search-api); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api)
- Parallel: cost per 1,000 questions = per-question average of LLM token cost + tool-call cost × 1,000, log scale; list prices $1 per 1k requests (Fast/Turbo), $5 (Basic/Advanced); no latency figures on its page; it cites OpenBenchmarks for latency. — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- OpenBenchmarks: factual lookup reports mean latency, snippet tokens, list-price cost per 1,000 queries and cost per 1,000 *correct* answers; hard retrieval reports median task time, average search latency, median task tokens and median task cost; multi-hop reports median time, mean turns, median task cost; fastest-search board ranks by mean client-measured latency (Parallel turbo 348 ms, Exa instant 398 ms). Costs are pay-as-you-go list prices; TinyFish $0 but capped at 30 rpm. — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search); [Parallel citing OpenBenchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- AIMultiple: mean latency per call (Brave 669 ms … Parallel Pro 13.6 s) and total wait over a 5-call workflow (3 s to 68 s); list prices per 1k requests. — [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search)
- Brave: single-search responses stream in <4.5 s on average; multi-search median 24.2 s; across the benchmark average 7 searches, 210 unique pages, 74 s; p90 LLM-context overhead <130 ms (Feb 2026 launch). — [Brave AI Grounding](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/); [Brave Feb 2026 launch](https://brave.com/blog/most-powerful-search-api-for-ai/)
- fastCRW: 4.12 searches and 2.06 fetches per task, 42.4 s wall time, $2.84 per 1,000 tasks for its own product vs $8.41 for the cheapest board entry ([VENDOR]). — [fastCRW](https://fastcrw.com/blog/aa-search-index-omniscience-benchmark)
- Exa docs: Fast ≈500 ms, Auto ≈1 s; compare against the closest competitor by P50. — [Exa docs](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/evaluating-exa-search)
- xbench-DeepSearch 2510 reports cost/task and time/task bands for agent products (e.g., ChatGPT-5-Pro ~$0.085, 5–8 min). — [xbench-evals](https://github.com/xbench-ai/xbench-evals)
- Keenable NEEDLE: Keenable-realtime median latency ~193 ms (via secondary coverage). — [search summary of MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/31/keenable-ai-open-sources-needle-a-live-search-benchmark-that-rebuilds-its-query-set-every-hour/)

### Inferences
- Per-call latency and per-task latency can invert: Exa fast is faster per query than Exa auto but slower-to-complete and costlier because the agent searches more (AA); Firecrawl is cheap per task but slowest per task. Buyers comparing "p50 of one call" against "time per agent task" are comparing different quantities.
- Because AA's model time is derived from token counts rather than measured, its Time-per-task is a modelled estimate; only the search component is a measurement.

### Gaps
- No evaluator publishes p95/p99 for *agent task* duration; only Perplexity and You.com publish request percentiles, and both are vendors.
- No source reports cost normalised per *correct answer* across an agent loop except OpenBenchmarks' factual-lookup board.

## Key Question 6: Methodology critiques of vendor-run (and other) benchmarks

### Takeaway
Critiques cluster around five issues: self-grading and circularity (Google writing DeepSearchQA; every vendor topping its own table), unpublished or inconsistent harnesses (judge, tool budget, sample IDs, best-of-N), tiny samples (50–100 questions), judge error and contamination (Brave's 167/292 judge errors; leaked benchmark pages), and human-preference artefacts (Search Arena's citation-count and length effects).

### Cited Findings
- Circularity: Google released Gemini Deep Research and DeepSearchQA together and topped its own leaderboard; implicator.ai calls it "a ruler that measures 11 inches" and "a marketing exercise dressed as science", noting testimonials came from GV (Google's VC arm) and that models not designed as research agents (Anthropic's) were scored on a task they weren't built for. — [implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/google-turns-research-agents-into-infrastructure-and-writes-the-test-theyre-graded-on). The SimpleQA Verified authors likewise note several are at Google and Gemini 2.5 Pro both tops the table and was used in filtering. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Unpublished harness details (Parallel, self-acknowledged): judge model and prompt, tool-call budget, sampled question IDs, system prompt, runs per configuration, run-to-run variance, contamination filtering and WideSearch item-matching rules are all unpublished; "we make the Parallel Search API and grade our own product." — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis)
- Mixed-provenance comparison tables: Linkup's 2025 post used competitors' self-published numbers rather than re-running them. — [Linkup blog](https://www.linkup.so/blog/linkup-establishes-sota-performance-on-simpleqa). SerpApi's table copies Tavily's and Parallel's reports and states they "give context, not a direct comparison." — [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5). Brave's competitor rows mix F1 and Acc* from different vendors' runs. — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/). Perplexity represents all "SERP based" providers by Tavily alone. — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- Best-of-N and tuning: Firecrawl reported "the better of two sessions." — [Firecrawl glossary](https://www.firecrawl.dev/glossary/web-search-apis/simpleqa-benchmark). LiveResearchBench configured each system to maximize performance. — [LiveResearchBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.14240). Tavily's repo does not disclose sample sizes, runs, variance or whether competitor parameters matched. — [tavily-search-evals](https://github.com/tavily-ai/tavily-search-evals)
- Small samples: Parallel's 50 BrowseComp questions imply ~±14 points at 95%; SE 6.5/4.6/3.2 at n=50/100/200. — [Parallel benchmarks](https://parallel.ai/articles/how-we-evaluate-web-search-apis); [Parallel BrowseComp explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark). OpenBenchmarks multi-hop has 45 questions. — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search). AA publishes no confidence intervals. — [TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/search-api-benchmark)
- Judge error: Brave's human review found the LLM judge wrong in 167 of 292 answers it marked incorrect. — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/). Exa notes LLM grading has "higher cost, more grading variance, dependence on the grading prompt" and a "verification gap." — [Exa evals](https://exa.ai/blog/evals-at-exa). Search Arena's experts agreed with crowd votes only 68% of the time. — [Search Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05334v2). TREC RAG 2025 found automatic relevance-judgment runs reached only 0.30–0.34 agreement with NIST assessors. — [TREC 2025 RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09891v1). Keenable's NEEDLE judge has no human-agreement audit. — [Parallel Keenable comparison](https://parallel.ai/articles/keenable-vs-parallel)
- Contamination: BrowseComp "questions still leak" (verbatim pages found). — [Parallel BrowseComp explainer](https://parallel.ai/articles/browsecomp-benchmark). Brave and SerpApi filtered leaked SimpleQA pages; AA filters canary strings and benchmark-hosted URLs; FinSearchComp reference answers are published online. — [Brave blog](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/); [SerpApi post](https://dev.to/adarshdigievo/serpapi-achieves-976-accuracy-on-simpleqa-verified-1mi5); [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/search-api); [Parallel FinSearchComp](https://parallel.ai/articles/finsearchcomp-benchmark.md). LiveBrowseComp argues standard decontamination misses "Intrinsic Knowledge Dependence." — [LiveBrowseComp](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.28721)
- Query selection bias: AIMultiple's queries are all AI/LLM topics from its own traffic and may not generalise; SerpApi, a tested vendor, is a subscriber to AIMultiple's services. — [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search). OpenBenchmarks' factual board shows "later-benchmarked vendors score higher for reasons unrelated to quality." — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search). Its operators and funding are undisclosed. — [OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/web-search)
- Rater bias in the benchmark itself: SimpleQA's narrow sources and redundancy trace to "substantial human rater biases" and "misaligned rater incentives" with no rater IDs released. — [SimpleQA Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07968v1)
- Human-preference artefacts: Search Arena users reward length (β=0.334) and citation count (β=0.209) and reward irrelevant citations almost as much as supporting ones (0.27 vs 0.29); authors call the analysis correlational and note demographic skew. — [Search Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05334v2)
- Live-API irreproducibility: BrowseComp-Plus authors argue live search APIs are "dynamic and opaque", making results irreproducible and conflating retriever and reasoner. — [BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06600v1). DeepSearchQA acknowledges ground truth can go stale. — [DeepSearchQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20975v1). Mind2Web 2 and LiveResearchBench results depend on the evaluation date. — [Mind2Web 2 project](https://osu-nlp-group.github.io/Mind2Web-2); [LiveResearchBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.14240)
- Configuration-as-product caveat: AA tests specific modes (Firecrawl `scrape_format=none`, Exa highlights, Perplexity context sizes); "a Search Index score doesn't reflect a vendor's whole product." — [TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/search-api-benchmark); [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/search-api)
- Round-up observation: "No single search API wins every AI benchmark" — Parallel led AA at launch, Firecrawl led a separate agentic index, Brave led AIMultiple; winners depend on the test. — [Keenable Select roundup](https://select.keenable.ai/r/ai-web-search-api-benchmarks/OaFKKMHeqBfx3jQKy9nI-w); [guptadeepak roundup](https://guptadeepak.com/research/web-search-apis-ai-agents-2026/)
- General (non-search-specific) critiques of vendor-run benchmarks: self-reported numbers "bake the vendor's best scaffold into the score"; recommended countermeasures include juries of judges, calibration against human labels and post-cutoff task construction. — [digitalapplied methodology guide](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/llm-benchmark-methodology-2026-contamination-leaderboard-guide); [Harness-vs-model paper, arXiv 2609.11987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11987)

### Inferences
- The only cross-vendor results that satisfy "same harness, same judge, same date, published settings" are AA's and OpenBenchmarks'; even these lack confidence intervals (AA) or disclosed operators (OpenBenchmarks). Every vendor table should be read as an upper bound for that vendor under its best configuration.
- Independent replication is feasible but rare: fastCRW's rebuild landed within 1.5 points of AA on one component, suggesting AA's published constants are sufficient to approximate the harness even with a different grading rubric.

### Gaps
- No Hacker News thread specifically critiquing search-API benchmark marketing was found; critiques located are blog posts, vendor self-disclosures and academic papers.
- No source audits AA's contamination filter effectiveness (what fraction of results were dropped per provider).
- No independent human-agreement audit of the GPT-5.6 Luna grader used by AA, or of Parallel's unnamed judge, was found.
