# Common Crawl vs. commercial web-search indexes (as of 2026-11-05)

Scope: what Common Crawl (CC) is and is not, quantified; the gaps commercial search APIs (Exa, Keenable, Brave, Google, Bing, Perplexity, Mojeek) fill; projects that built search on CC and what they learned; comparative index sizes and recrawl rates; LLM-era use of CC and the CCBot-blocking debate; cost of indexing a CC snapshot.

Conventions: every figure is dated. "Measured" = published by the operator from its own data or by an independent study; "vendor claim" = marketing statement without methodology. Research note: the shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through this task, so the last third of the work used direct fetches of known primary URLs only; gaps that resulted are listed per section.

---

## KQ1. Common Crawl facts: scale, cadence, URL selection, new-vs-revisited, politeness, formats, indexes, access latency, diversity, biases

### Takeaway
A CC main crawl in 2026 is ~2.0–2.2 billion fetched pages (~350–380 TiB uncompressed) from ~40–45 million hosts, released roughly monthly; this is down ~29% from ~3.0B pages per crawl in early 2025. The frontier is a sample-of-samples driven by harmonic-centrality ranking of domains, with a fixed per-crawl URL cap, per-domain budgets scaled by centrality, a politeness policy that deliberately under-represents large/popular sites, no JavaScript execution, robots.txt compliance, and a US-based crawler that skews toward English (42.6% of pages in Oct 2026). CC ships raw WARC/WAT/WET files plus two URL-lookup indexes (CDXJ and a Parquet "URL Index") on S3; there is no full-text search, and the public CDX API is heavily rate-limited.

- Fetch-size truncation: archived content is truncated at 1 MiB before CC-MAIN-2025-13 (March 2025) and 5 MiB from that crawl onward. — [Common Crawl, October 2026 Crawl Archive (erratum)](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/october-2026-crawl-archive-now-available)

**How a crawl's URLs are chosen (measured/interview-based)**
- Seed donations (notably from the search startup blekko, acquired by IBM in 2015) were the main URL source 2013–2016; after they dwindled, CC (inspired by the discontinued Common Search project) built an approach ranking web domains by harmonic centrality, chosen over PageRank because "it is deemed better at avoiding spam". Scores decide both which domains are crawled and how many URLs per domain ("the higher the domain's score, the more URLs"); within a domain, the domain score is projected to its URLs and the highest-ranking are kept. — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- Each crawl starts from a CrawlDB of ~25 billion URLs (August 2023) recording score, last-fetch timestamp and status; CrawlDB is expanded from prior crawls, separate discovery crawls and sitemaps. Selection combines harmonic centrality with bonuses/penalties based on time since last successful fetch; the score threshold is adjusted to hit "the set maximum of URLs for one crawl"; per-domain URL counts depend on the domain's harmonic centrality. — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- CC director on why the crawl is capped: "When we stop at three billion, we think we're getting a pretty good sampling. If we were to 10X that, we believe the quality would drop because we're going into lower ranked pages." He calls the data "an academic sampling of the web". — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- CC "never collects full copies of domains. There are no complete copies of Wikipedia in Common Crawl"; it collects HTML only, generally not CSS, images or media. — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- CC FAQ: "we do not generally archive any entire website but a randomly selected subset of it"; crawl candidates are "sorted by host (domain name) and then distributed to a set of crawler servers"; CCBot uses sitemaps announced in robots.txt. — [Common Crawl FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq)
- Web graph ranking: CC publishes host- and domain-level web graphs, each covering a 3-month window shifting by one month; latest release cc-main-2026-aug-sep-oct (page updated 2026-10-22); hosts/domains are ranked by Harmonic Centrality (HyperBall) with PageRank for comparison; the stats page says "Harmonic Centrality is better for reducing this spam". — [CC webgraph statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-webgraph-statistics/); [CC web graphs](https://commoncrawl.org/web-graphs)
- On March 6, 2026 CC added a lookup tool to plot harmonic centrality and PageRank over time for any of the top 1,000 domains. — [Common Crawl, Web Graph Statistics Gets a Proper Upgrade](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/web-graph-statistics-gets-a-proper-upgrade)

**New vs revisited**
- "about 50% of each crawl is made up of URLs that have been fetched in previous crawls (Interview CC Crawl Engineer)"; captures are "discontinuous; what happens in between two different crawls is not captured". — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- Measured new-URL shares in 2026: June 2026 479M new of 2.10B (≈23%); October 2026 830.1M new of 2.16B (≈38%). — [June 2026](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/june-2026-crawl-archive-now-available); [October 2026](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/october-2026-crawl-archive-now-available)
- CC's statistics site splits captures into new URLs, revisits (archived in an earlier crawl) and within-crawl duplicates; unique-URL/content estimates use HyperLogLog with ~1% error; crawl size varies with "changes in available seeds, the schedule for revisiting pages, and crawler operating issues"; coverage of combined recent crawls dropped in early 2015 when seed donations stopped and CC has "extended crawling itself" since autumn 2016. — [CC crawl size statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/crawlsize)
- Content overlap between crawls (Jaccard of content digests) is "small and in the same order of magnitude as the 1% error rate" of the estimates. — [CC crawl overlap statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/crawloverlap)

**robots.txt, politeness, JavaScript**
- CCBot fetches robots.txt first and only fetches allowed pages; follows up to 4 redirects (5 for robots.txt) per RFC 9309; honors `Crawl-delay`; "By default our crawler waits few seconds before sending the next request to the same site"; adaptive back-off on HTTP 429/5xx; "Currently, JavaScript is not executed and Cookies are not used." — [Common Crawl FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq)
- User-agent `CCBot/2.0 (https://commoncrawl.org/faq/)`; runs from dedicated IP ranges with reverse DNS under crawl.commoncrawl.org, published at index.commoncrawl.org/ccbot.json; CC warns other crawlers spoof the CCBot UA. — [Common Crawl CCBot page](https://commoncrawl.org/ccbot)
- "We do not scrape paywalled material"; CCBot does not log in or evade access restrictions. — [Common Crawl, Setting the Record Straight, Nov 4, 2025](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/setting-the-record-straight-common-crawls-commitment-to-transparency-fair-use-and-the-public-good)
- Consequence of politeness: CC's domain stats page says the crawler "respects the robots.txt and tries hard not to overload web servers" and therefore "Highly ranked domains tend to be underrepresented". — [CC domain statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/domains)

**Formats and indexes**
- WARC = raw HTTP requests/responses + crawl metadata; WAT = computed metadata per WARC record (HTTP headers, extracted links) as JSON; WET = extracted plaintext. Access via `s3://commoncrawl/` (us-east-1), `https://data.commoncrawl.org/` (no AWS account needed), a CloudFront prefix, and a Hugging Face storage-bucket mirror for selected crawls. — [Common Crawl Get Started](https://commoncrawl.org/get-started)
- On June 3, 2026 the "Columnar Index" was renamed the "URL Index": Apache Parquet, at `s3://commoncrawl/cc-index/table/cc-main/warc/`, queryable with Athena, Spark, Pandas, Polars, Arrow, DuckDB; "well suited to efficient analytical and bulk queries"; it sits alongside the CDXJ index. — [Common Crawl, The Columnar Index Is Now the URL Index](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/the-columnar-index-is-now-the-url-index)
- URL Index columns include `url`, `content_languages`, `crawl`, `subset`, `warc_filename`, `warc_record_offset`, `warc_record_length`; `CCIndexWarcExport` fetches matching WARC records by offset/length. — [commoncrawl/cc-index-table](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-index-table)
- The public CDX server (index.commoncrawl.org) is a pywb deployment answering URL lookups; most of the index is served from S3 with a small local secondary index. — [commoncrawl/cc-index-server](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-index-server)
- "Our CDX API endpoint is frequently abused and therefore heavily rate limited"; blocked IPs should "wait 24 hours"; large-scale queries should use the URL Index via Athena or Spark. — [Common Crawl FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq)
- FineWeb authors found WET files "retained too much boilerplate and menu text" and instead extracted text from WARC HTML with trafilatura, which produced better models in ablations. — [FineWeb paper, arXiv 2406.17557 (June 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2)

**Host/domain diversity and concentration (measured, CC-MAIN-2026-43)**
- Top registered domains by captured pages: blogspot.com 15,831,566 (0.73% of pages; 190,619 hosts); wikipedia.org 3,033,002 (0.14%); wordpress.org 2,807,142; google.com 1,563,267; made-in-china.com 1,499,750; wiktionary.org 1,341,621; aif.ru 1,151,356; europa.eu 1,083,839; godaddy.com 1,069,459; yandex.ru 896,567. — [CC domain statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/domains)

**Language distribution (measured, CLD2 primary language, CC-MAIN-2026-43)**
- English 42.56%, Russian 6.30%, German 5.77%, Japanese 4.89%, French 4.76%, Spanish 4.74%, Chinese 4.37%, Italian 2.58%, Portuguese 2.47%, unknown 3.06%. English rose from 40.45% (2026-34) to 41.86% (2026-39) to 42.56% (2026-43). — [CC language statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/languages)
- Mozilla: "The language and regional coverage is strongly skewed toward English content"; CC's US-based infrastructure "skews crawls toward English content (for example, pages providing multiple languages may serve the English version)". — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)

**Known biases and limits (stated by CC staff or measured)**
- "Common Crawl does not contain the 'entire web,' nor a representative sample of it." Crawl engineer: "Based on what I know about how many URLs exist, it's very, very small." Facebook and The New York Times block most or all crawling. — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- Spam/crawler traps: CC uses heuristics plus manual intervention; the director describes a trap where "80% of it is a particular domain park with a bunch of junk pages". — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)
- Server-side refusals (independent study of CC-MAIN-2023-50, among sites that did not disallow CCBot): ~800,000 sites (1.68% of sites in the snapshot) returned explicit refusals; 31 status codes, mainly 403, 406, 429, 430 (Shopify); ModSecurity accounted for 70% of refusing domains, 92% of those on one hosting conglomerate (Newfold Digital) with a centralized CCBot block; ~80% of blocking sites block on every request; 429 was used for non-transient refusals by 40% of sites using it. Presented 23 July 2025 (Ansar, Sperotto, Holz; IETF 123 MAPRG). — [IETF 123 MAPRG slides](https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/123/materials/slides-123-maprg-sessa-web-crawl-refusals-insights-from-common-crawl)
- Academic composition findings on C4 (built from a single CC snapshot): large shares from unexpected sources (patents, US military sites), machine-translated text, benchmark contamination; blocklist filtering disproportionately removes text about minorities (Dodge et al., EMNLP 2021). — [arXiv 2104.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08758)
- CC also operates a separate, more frequently updated news crawl (CC-NEWS), rarely used by LLM builders (1 of 47 models surveyed). — [Mozilla Foundation report PDF, Feb 2024](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2026 crawls (~2.1–2.2B pages) are roughly 30% smaller than the "three to five billion" norm CC itself described for 2017–2023; the Bright Data analysis is explicit that cause is unidentified, so the drop should not be attributed solely to CCBot blocking.
- Because ~50% of each crawl is revisits (Mozilla) and measured new-URL shares were 23–38% in 2026, the cumulative unique-URL count grows far slower than "2B pages per month" suggests; a "fresh" CC snapshot is best thought of as ~0.5–0.8B never-seen URLs plus ~1.3–1.6B re-fetches.
- The host counts (40–45M hosts, 33–37M registered domains per crawl) against ~2.1B pages imply a median of a few dozen pages per host; CC is wide (many hosts) and shallow (few pages per host), by design.

### Gaps
- CC publishes no numeric per-host/per-domain budget; only the qualitative rule (budget scales with harmonic centrality, fixed per-crawl cap). The exact cap is not published.
- No official CC statement of total archive size in PB after mid-2023 (9.5 PB via Mozilla); CC's own pages say only "petabytes"/"many petabytes".
- Percentages of new vs revisited URLs are only available as per-crawl announcements (two data points gathered here) or in CSVs behind the statistics charts, which were not readable via fetch.
- Current CC-NEWS cadence/size was not retrieved.

## KQ2. What Common Crawl does NOT provide that a search API does

### Takeaway
CC is a static, monthly, sampled archive of raw HTML with URL-lookup indexes; everything that makes a search API usable at query time — inverted index or embeddings, ranking signals (link graph beyond host-level centrality, clicks, spam/quality classifiers, dedup), freshness measured in hours, query understanding, snippet/passage extraction, JS rendering, structured fields, and ~100s-of-ms serving — must be built by the consumer. Commercial providers document exactly these layers.

### Cited Findings
- No full-text retrieval: CC's two indexes are URL/metadata lookups (CDXJ server; Parquet URL Index with url, languages, crawl, subset, WARC file/offset/length columns) — [cc-index-table](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-index-table); [cc-index-server](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-index-server); [URL Index rename, June 3, 2026](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/the-columnar-index-is-now-the-url-index). Elastic ChatNoir exists precisely because "none of them have been deployed at web scale, and optimized for fast retrieval, and made publicly available for free" — [Bevendorff et al., ECIR 2018](https://downloads.webis.de/publications/papers/bevendorff_2018.pdf)
- Freshness: CC snapshots are monthly and "discontinuous" — [Mozilla report](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf); OWI contrasts: "Unlike Common Crawl, we crawl on a daily basis ... index delta files ... at daily intervals, as opposed to monthly or bi-monthly data dumps" — [Hendriksen et al. 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/210780/1/hendriksen_2024.pdf). Google: crawlers "learn how often content they've seen before seems to change and revisit as needed" — [Google How Search Works](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/organizing-information/). Brave (vendor claim): "over one hundred million new and refreshed pages each day" — [Brave API guide, updated Oct 16, 2025](https://brave.com/search/api/guides/what-sets-brave-search-api-apart/). Perplexity (vendor claim): "tens of thousands of indexing operations per second"; an ML model decides which URLs to index and when — [Perplexity, Sept 25, 2025](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)
- JavaScript rendering: CC "JavaScript is not executed" — [CC FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq); Google "render the content of the page, just as a browser does" — [Google How Search Works](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/organizing-information/); ClueWeb22 (built from a commercial engine's pipeline) includes "a visual rendering of each page produced by a web browser" — [arXiv 2211.15848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15848)
- Ranking signals: CC publishes only host/domain-level harmonic centrality and PageRank — [CC webgraph statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-webgraph-statistics/). Perplexity describes multi-stage ranking: lexical + semantic candidate retrieval, prefilters, lexical and embedding scorers, cross-encoder rerankers, at document and sub-document level — [Perplexity, Sept 25, 2025](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api). Brave (vendor claim) cites signals from "130+ million real humans" via the opt-in Web Discovery Project — [Brave Search API page](https://brave.com/search/api/). Google indexes "key signals - from keywords to website freshness" — [Google](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/organizing-information/). MS MARCO Web Search shows the click-label layer: 10M Bing queries in 93 languages with real click labels over 10B ClueWeb22 docs — [arXiv 2405.07526](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07526v1)
- Spam/quality/dedup/boilerplate: CC deliberately does minimal curation ("less curation ... enables more research"); contains hate speech, pornography, "boiler-plate text like menus, error messages, or duplicate text"; spam only removed to avoid crawler traps — [Mozilla report](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf). FineWeb: base filtering leaves ~36T tokens, per-snapshot MinHash ~20T, heuristics remove a further ~22%, final ~15T; global dedup across 96 snapshots removed up to 90% of the oldest snapshots and left worse-quality data — [FineWeb paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2). ChatNoir's pipeline had to extract main content, detect language, map external spam ranks, and discard documents with no useful content before indexing — [Bevendorff et al. 2018](https://downloads.webis.de/publications/papers/bevendorff_2018.pdf)
- Coverage of high-value sites: CC's politeness means "Highly ranked domains tend to be underrepresented" — [CC domain stats](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/domains); CC never archives whole sites — [CC FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq); major news/tech publishers block CCBot (see KQ5).
- Snippets/passages: Exa describes a model that extracts relevant excerpts ("highlights") — [exa.ai](https://exa.ai/); Keenable markets "Dynamic Snippets" with "50% Fewer Tokens" (Oct 15, 2026) — [keenable.ai](https://keenable.ai/); Perplexity ranks at sub-document level — [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api). CC offers WET plaintext only, which FineWeb found boilerplate-heavy — [FineWeb paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2)
- Serving latency (measured by Perplexity from AWS us-east-1, Sept 2025): Perplexity p50 358 ms / p95 763 ms; Brave p50 513 / p95 808; Exa p50 1,375 / p95 2,188; Tavily (SERP-based) p50 1,342 / p95 1,790 — [Perplexity, Sept 25, 2025](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api). Brave cites AIMultiple averages: Brave 669 ms, Tavily 998, Exa 1,200, Firecrawl 1,335, SerpApi 2,400, Perplexity 11,000 (undated) — [Brave Search API page](https://brave.com/search/api/). Keenable (vendor claim): "<250ms p95 (US East)" — [keenable.ai](https://keenable.ai/). By contrast, CC's own CDX API is "heavily rate limited" — [CC FAQ](https://commoncrawl.org/faq), and a 2018 experiment noted "Random-seek latency makes S3-hosted querying costly" — [fulmicoton.com, Feb 2018](https://fulmicoton.com/posts/commoncrawl/)
- Access terms: CC data is free under its Terms of Use; the S3 bucket is in us-east-1 and cross-region egress is at the user's expense — [CC Get Started](https://commoncrawl.org/get-started); [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/commoncrawl/)

### Inferences
- The missing layers map onto the cost centers later sections quantify: storage/indexing (ChatNoir, tantivy), continuous recrawl (OWI, Brave, Perplexity), and ranking data (Bing click logs in MS MARCO Web Search; Brave WDP). None of these is derivable from a CC snapshot alone.
- The latency figures suggest that even well-funded embedding-first providers (Exa) run at >1 s p50; CC-based hobby engines can match BM25 latency (ChatNoir ~600 ms warm) but not the ranking data.

### Gaps
- No provider publishes structured-field extraction (dates, authors) coverage statistics; only qualitative marketing was found.
- No independent, apples-to-apples freshness benchmark across providers was found (Brave's FreshQA numbers are self-run).

## KQ3. Projects that built search on Common Crawl — and what they learned

### Takeaway
Every serious CC-based engine either stayed academic (ChatNoir: 1.6B CC pages on 124 Elasticsearch data nodes, ~116 TB on disk, ~600 ms warm queries), used CC only as a bootstrap before writing its own crawler (Stract, archived April 2026), or abandoned CC-size generality for a focused/own crawl (Marginalia ~$200/month single server; OWI's thesis that "it is often cheaper to crawl and index only a smaller collection ... than to build and maintain an index for a general collection the size of the Common Crawl"). CC's dominant downstream success is as LLM pretraining corpora (C4, RefinedWeb, FineWeb, DCLM), not as a search index; the Bing-derived ClueWeb22/MS MARCO Web Search show what a commercial pipeline adds (rendering, click labels).

### Cited Findings

**Elastic ChatNoir (Webis; ECIR 2018; still running 2026)**
- Indexed ClueWeb09 (734.8M docs), ClueWeb12 (638.8M), Common Crawl 11/2015 (1.6B) = 3.0B documents; cluster "Betaweb" 130 nodes (124 data, 5 master/coord, 1 monitoring); 40 primary shards per corpus, replication 3; CC index 9.7 TB (29.2 TB replicated), CC document map files 28.8 TB (86.4 TB replicated), CC total on disk 115.6 TB; whole system 172.6 TB; RAM cache 150 GB; avg query ~600 ms warm, ~2,100 ms cold; indexing throughput ≥20,000 docs/s on 124 data nodes; BM25 with Boolean prefilter then top-400-per-shard rescoring; 8 HDDs per data node; continuous cache warming with the AOL query log. Pipeline: parse WARC, deduce content type/encoding, main-content extraction, language detection, metadata extraction, external spam ranks, discard docs with no useful content. — [Bevendorff, Stein, Hagen, Potthast, ECIR 2018](https://downloads.webis.de/publications/papers/bevendorff_2018.pdf)
- TREC Web track 2013/2014 effectiveness: default ChatNoir ERR@20/nDCG@20 0.130/0.193 (2013) and 0.195/0.249 (2014); optimized 0.134/0.197 and 0.213/0.254 — above Terrier/Indri baselines, mid-pack vs participants. — [same paper](https://downloads.webis.de/publications/papers/bevendorff_2018.pdf)
- Current scope: ChatNoir indexes ClueWeb09, ClueWeb12, ClueWeb22, MS MARCO and "a periodically updated set of Common Crawl snapshots"; "billions of indexed web pages"; indexes fixed snapshots, not the live web (© 2026). — [Webis ChatNoir page](https://webis.de/research/chatnoir.html)

**tantivy / "Of using Common Crawl to play Family Feud" (Feb 23, 2018; individual experiment)**

- Thesis: "The public availability of the large-scale Common Crawl does not alleviate the situation, as it is often cheaper to crawl and index only a smaller collection focused on a downstream application scenario than to build and maintain an index for a general collection the size of the Common Crawl." - C4: built from "a single snapshot of Common Crawl" (Dodge et al. 2021). — [arXiv 2104.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08758)
- RefinedWeb: "five trillion tokens from CommonCrawl", 600B released, filtering + dedup only (June 2023). — [arXiv 2306.01116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01116)
- FineWeb: 96 CC snapshots (2013-48 → 2024), ~15T tokens; FineWeb-Edu 1.3T; ablations used ~80,000 H100 GPU-hours (70+ models); classifying 15T tokens with the Edu classifier ~6,000 H100 GPU-hours (NeurIPS 2024). — [arXiv 2406.17557](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2); [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/370df50ccfdf8bde18f8f9c2d9151bda-Abstract.html)
- DCLM: "a standardized corpus of 240T tokens extracted from Common Crawl" (DCLM-Pool); 7B model trained on 2.6T tokens of DCLM-Baseline (June 2024, v4 Apr 2025). — [arXiv 2406.11794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11794)
- ClueWeb22: 10 billion pages "aligned with document distributions in commercial web search", with raw HTML, browser renderings, parsed structure, cleaned text (Nov 2022). — [arXiv 2211.15848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15848)
- MS MARCO Web Search: 10B ClueWeb22 documents + 10M unique Bing queries in 93 languages with real click labels; 100M-doc random subset provided; non-commercial research only. — [arXiv 2405.07526](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07526v1)
- Mozilla: CC was >80% of GPT-3 tokens; at least 30 of 47 text-generation LLMs (64%) published 2019–Oct 2023 used a filtered CC version; CC's homepage briefly advertised "82% of raw tokens used to train GPT-3" (Aug 2023). — [Mozilla report](https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/Common_Crawl_Mozilla_Foundation_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lessons converge: (1) raw CC needs heavy cleaning (main-content extraction, language ID, dedup, spam) before it is index-worthy; (2) a 2015-scale CC snapshot needed ~100+ TB of disk and >100 nodes for sub-second BM25 at research load; (3) projects that wanted freshness or site completeness (Stract, Marginalia, OWI) wrote their own crawlers; (4) CC's comparative advantage is bulk, offline, batch processing (LLM corpora), not online retrieval.
- Scaling ChatNoir's 2015 ratios (1.6B pages → 9.7 TB index + 28.8 TB doc store before replication) to a 2026 crawl (2.16B pages, with 5 MiB truncation vs 1 MiB) suggests a BM25 index on the order of 13–20 TB plus a 40+ TB document store per snapshot before replication; this is an extrapolation, not a measured figure.

- www.alexandria.org could not be resolved from this session's network; Alexandria's own index size, hardware and cost figures are therefore missing.
- Marginalia's current document count (2025–2026) was not found in primary sources; a secondary site claims 300–400M (2024) and a Jan 2026 HN comment ~1.1B, both unverified.
- ChatNoir's currently indexed CC snapshot IDs and cluster size in 2026 were not retrievable (chatnoir.eu returned only a title).
- OWI's current document count is not published in text form (dashboard pages fetched empty).
- No "Common Crawl + Elasticsearch" cost write-ups beyond ChatNoir and the tantivy post were found before the search budget ran out.

## KQ4. How large are commercial indexes by comparison, and how fast do they recrawl?

A single CC crawl (~2.2B pages) is 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller than these claims and is refreshed monthly rather than continuously.

- Counting units differ (pages served vs documents vs URLs tracked vs vectors), so cross-vendor rankings are unreliable; the Keenable review of Exa makes this explicit, and Exa's own CEO concedes it.

The cumulative CC archive (300B+ captures, not unique pages) is in Google's order of magnitude only if duplicates/revisits are ignored.
- Recrawl cadence gap: CC's revisit interval is monthly at best and most URLs are not revisited at all (~50% revisits per crawl); Brave's claimed 100M+ updates/day and Perplexity's "tens of thousands of indexing operations per second" imply continuous recrawl.
- worldwidewebsize.com's ~4B estimate illustrates why index-size statistics are contested: its method measures the result-count-visible web, not the stored index.

### Gaps
- No official Bing or Yandex index totals were found; the search budget was exhausted before Yandex could be researched.
- No independent audit of Brave, Exa, Keenable or Perplexity index sizes exists in the sources found.
- Google's page is undated; the "100,000,000 GB" figure has been on it for years and may be stale.

## KQ5. How LLM-era search startups use Common Crawl; CC's statements on AI; the 2025–2026 CCBot-blocking debate

### Takeaway
Documented CC use among search engines is limited to bootstrapping (Stract's initial index, Alexandria, OWI's planned ingestion); Exa, Keenable, Brave, Mojeek and Perplexity all say they run their own crawlers and none was found stating that it uses CC. Meanwhile CC itself has leaned into its LLM role ("82% of raw tokens used to train GPT-3", an "AI Visibility Audit" for SEOs) and is treated by robots.txt trackers as an "AI Data Scraper": 26% of the top 1,000 sites had CCBot rules on Nov 4, 2026; 37% of 107 prominent publishers blocked it in June 2026; it ranks second after Bytespider on block-to-allow ratio in Cloudflare Radar data (Sep 2026); many news/tech publishers block it outright, and CC publicly rebutted an Atlantic exposé in Nov 2025.

- The visible CC-to-search pipeline in 2026 is bootstrap → own crawler: both engines documented as CC-based (Stract, Alexandria) either moved off CC or are small; well-funded LLM-era providers (Exa $250M Series C; Keenable $26M seed) position proprietary crawling as the moat.
- Blocking is concentrated in exactly the content a search API needs most (news, tech press, large platforms), so a CC-based engine inherits a systematic hole in high-authority, time-sensitive content rather than a random 10–26% loss.
- Because CCBot is categorized as an AI-training scraper by Cloudflare and the trackers, its block rate is set by the AI-training debate, not by search-engine economics; this is a structural disadvantage versus crawlers that sites perceive as sending traffic (Googlebot) or as search-only (PerplexityBot's 1.07:1 block-to-allow ratio vs CCBot's 2.61:1).

### Gaps
- No primary statement from Exa, Keenable, Brave, Mojeek or Perplexity about using (or not using) CC for seeds, training rankers or embeddings; absence of mention is not denial.
- CC's Opt-Out Ledger count (number of domains/publishers) is only in a linked spreadsheet not readable here.
- The share of CC-derived pages from sites that later opted out, and the measured impact of blocking on crawl size, are not published by CC; Bright Data's analysis explicitly declines to attribute the 29% decline.

## KQ6. Cost model: indexing one CC snapshot (BM25 or embeddings) and serving it

### Takeaway
Published figures are sparse and dated, but consistent: a BM25 index of a full CC snapshot is on the order of 10–20 TB (tantivy 2018: ~13.2 TB for 3.2B pages; ChatNoir 2018: 9.7 TB for 1.6B pages plus 28.8 TB document store), costs tens of dollars of batch compute to build but hundreds of dollars per month just to store on S3, and needs 100+ nodes and ~150 GB of cache to serve sub-second at research load. Embedding a snapshot has no published end-to-end cost figure; the closest proxy is FineWeb's 6,000 H100-hours to run a small classifier over 15T tokens.

### Cited Findings
- **Compute to build a BM25 index (2018 estimates, individual):** ~875 instance-hours (t2.medium-equivalent) to index ~17 TB of WET text; EC2 estimate "less than $50" using ~80 instances at ~$0.05; index ~13.2 TB (165 GB/shard × 80); S3 storage ~$400/month for 17 TB; home storage three or four 4 TB HDDs ≈ $255; download at 3–4 MB/s ≈ 34 days; author's caveat that earlier estimates were "not too far from reality" but the full index was never finished. — [fulmicoton.com, Feb 23, 2018](https://fulmicoton.com/posts/commoncrawl/)
- **Production-grade serving (academic, 2018):** 1.6B CC pages → 9.7 TB index + 28.8 TB document map files, ×3 replication → 115.6 TB on disk for the CC corpus alone; 124 data nodes with 8 HDDs each; 150 GB RAM cache; ≥20,000 docs/s indexing; ~600 ms warm / ~2,100 ms cold queries; cache warming needed to hit "a few hundred milliseconds". — [Bevendorff et al., ECIR 2018](https://downloads.webis.de/publications/papers/bevendorff_2018.pdf)
- **Raw data volume to process (2026):** one crawl = ~86 TiB compressed WARC (371 TiB uncompressed) or ~5.85 TiB compressed WET; WET is boilerplate-heavy so quality pipelines read WARC. — [October 2026 crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/october-2026-crawl-archive-now-available); [FineWeb paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2)
- **Egress/locality:** S3 access should be from us-east-1; HTTPS access without an AWS account exists; HF mirror for selected crawls with CDN pre-warming. — [CC Get Started](https://commoncrawl.org/get-started)
- **Cleaning cost proxies:** FineWeb ablations ~80,000 H100 GPU-hours (model training, not data processing); FineWeb-Edu classifier over 15T tokens ~6,000 H100 GPU-hours; datatrove processing CPU-hours not reported. — [FineWeb paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557v2)
- **Small independent engines:** Marginalia ≈ $200/month on one two-CPU NUMA server (own crawl, ~10^8-scale index). — [about.marginalia-search.com](https://about.marginalia-search.com/); [marginalia.nu, July 2026](https://www.marginalia.nu/log/a_138_systemdocker/). Mwmbl: cost "at least proportional to the number of items stored", donation-funded. — [mwmbl README](https://github.com/mwmbl/mwmbl/blob/main/README.md)
- **Public-infrastructure scale:** OWI: €8.5M over 42 months, two data centres each producing ~1 TB/day, >30M pages/day cumulative; the project's own abstract says building and maintaining an index for a CC-size general collection is usually more expensive than crawling a focused collection. — [OpenWebSearch.eu FAQ](https://openwebsearch.eu/faqs/); [Hendriksen et al. 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/210780/1/hendriksen_2024.pdf)
- **Commercial scale:** Exa raised a $250M Series C (May 2026) and reports 10k+ QPS vector databases; Keenable raised $26M and says building a large index is "painfully expensive"; Perplexity describes an "exabyte-scale" index on "tens of thousands of CPUs" (secondary summary). — [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/exa-search-index-80-billion-pages-google-scale-2027); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3156161); [mochi-lab analysis of Perplexity](https://mochi-lab.vercel.app/blog/ai-first-search)
- **Price floor set by commercial APIs:** Brave $5/1,000 (Search), Keenable $4/1,000 ($1/1,000 at 100+ RPS), Brave FreshQA cost $18/1,000 questions; Perplexity notes a legacy provider charged $200/1,000 in 2023. — [Brave Search API](https://brave.com/search/api/); [keenable.ai](https://keenable.ai/); [Perplexity blog](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/architecting-and-evaluating-an-ai-first-search-api)

### Inferences
- BM25 over one 2026 snapshot: scaling ChatNoir's 2015 ratio (6 KB index + 18 KB doc store per page) to 2.16B pages gives ~13 TB index + ~39 TB document store before replication (~150 TB at 3× replication); the 5 MiB truncation limit since March 2025 could push the document store higher. Build compute is cheap relative to storage; the recurring cost is disk, RAM for caching, and egress. (Extrapolation; no 2026 measurement exists.)
- Embeddings: 2.16B pages × even one 768-d float16 vector ≈ 3.3 TB of raw vectors before ANN index overhead; the GPU cost of producing them is unpublished for CC specifically. FineWeb's 6,000 H100-hours for a small classifier over ~15T tokens is a lower bound on the cost of running any transformer over a comparable token volume (one snapshot after FineWeb-style filtering is roughly 15T/96 ≈ 150B tokens, so a classifier-sized pass would be on the order of 60 H100-hours; a full embedding model is far heavier). These are arithmetic inferences from cited numbers, not published costs.
- The expensive parts are not in CC at all: continuous recrawl (Brave's claimed 100M+ pages/day vs CC's ~70M/day averaged), ranking data (clicks, user signals), and multi-region low-latency serving — which is why CC-based projects stay academic or hobby-scale and funded entrants build their own crawl.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end cost (GPU-hours or dollars) for embedding a full CC snapshot was found; Exa's and Keenable's blogs do not disclose it.
- No 2024–2026 "CC + Elasticsearch/OpenSearch/Vespa" write-up with current hardware costs was found before the search budget was exhausted; all production-grade numbers are from 2018.
