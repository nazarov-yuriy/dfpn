Headline. Four commercial providers say anything checkable about this layer: Exa (a query classifier and a freshness model at enrichment time, RRF fusion, then two rerankers, "relevance" and "authority", with authority-weighted final ranking, then Highlights; 2026-04-17), Perplexity (lexical and embedding scorers early, cross-encoder rerankers late, at document and sub-document level; 2025-09-25), Keenable (a query-conditioned token classifier for Dynamic Snippets with published thresholds; 2026-10-15; nothing on ranking itself) and Brave (LLM Context "smart chunks" with "data-first ranking", Goggles re-ranking, a token budget, p90 under 500 ms; 2026-02-12). Jina sells a reranker (jina-reranker-v3/v3.5, CC-BY-NC weights) and is the only provider whose reranker can be inspected. Parallel, Tavily, Linkup, Firecrawl, You.com, Octen, Ceramic, Mojeek and Kagi publish nothing checkable about ranking models. Among the open engines, Stract is the only one with a full modern ranking stack in a readable codebase (BM25/BM25F signals, inbound-link similarity, a linear model, a LambdaMART model and a cross-encoder in Rust, plus "optics" for user re-ranking), but it was archived on 2026-04-02; Marginalia ranks "broadly" with BM25 plus feature bonuses/penalties; Common Crawl ships only host/domain-level harmonic centrality and PageRank; Mwmbl lets the community curate rankings; ChatNoir is BM25 on Elasticsearch. The open-source equivalents for every component exist and are mostly Apache-2.0 or MIT, with two licence traps: the Jina rerankers and Provence are non-commercial, and several strong training datasets (MS MARCO Web Search) are research-only.

## KQ1. Component map: who uses what, the open-source equivalents, and what to learn

### Takeaway
Commercial disclosure is thin and concentrated in Exa, Perplexity, Keenable and Brave; the open-source stack (gradient-boosted LambdaMART in LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost, cross-encoders from BGE/mixedbread/Qwen/Jina, listwise LLM rerankers from castorini, Provence and LLMLingua-2 for passage pruning, BEIR/MTEB/ir_measures/ranx for evaluation) covers every component the vendors describe, and the only components with no credible open substitute are the proprietary training signals: agent trajectories (Keenable), click logs (Bing, Brave WDP) and LLM-labelled relevance at Exa's scale.

### Inferences
- The one architectural pattern every disclosing vendor shares is a cascade: cheap hybrid retrieval over thousands of candidates, learned or neural reranking over tens to hundreds, then a passage/token selector that decides what the agent reads. The open-source stack maps onto this one-to-one; what cannot be downloaded is the training signal and the serving engineering (Exa's ~250 ms DAG, Keenable's 200 ms p95, Brave's sub-500 ms p90, all vendor figures) [inferred from the table].
- Exa's "authority" reranker and Common Crawl's harmonic-centrality choice point the same way: link-derived trust is still a separate signal from text relevance even in neural stacks [inferred].

## KQ4. Listwise LLM reranking (RankGPT, RankZephyr, RankVicuna, FIRST) as used in the reader's proxy

## Learning path for the ranking, reranking, query-understanding, snippet and evaluation layer

1. **Measure first.** Learn qrels, nDCG@k, MRR and Recall@k with ir_measures or ranx and trec_eval; load collections through ir_datasets; reproduce a BM25 baseline with Pyserini or PyTerrier — [ranx](https://github.com/AmenRa/ranx); [ir_measures](https://github.com/terrierteam/ir_measures); [Pyserini](https://github.com/castorini/pyserini) (stats in KQ8).
2. **Gradient-boosted LTR.** Train LambdaMART with LightGBM (`lambdarank`) and CatBoost (`YetiRank`/`LambdaMart`) on query-grouped features (BM25/BM25F per field, URL and host features, link centrality, age, click or trajectory counts); read CatBoost's ranking objectives page and Stract's `ranking/models/lambdamart.rs` as a production example — [CatBoost docs](https://catboost.ai/docs/en/concepts/loss-functions-ranking); [Stract models](https://github.com/StractOrg/stract/tree/main/crates/core/src/ranking/models).
3. **Cross-encoders.** Fine-tune and serve a cross-encoder with sentence-transformers or FlagEmbedding; compare bge-reranker-v2-m3, gte-reranker-modernbert-base, mxbai-rerank-v2 and Qwen3-Reranker on your own candidate lists, not on card numbers; understand yes/no-token scoring and instruction prompts — [Qwen3-Reranker card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B); [bge-reranker card](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3).
4. **Listwise LLM reranking.** Run rank_llm with RankZephyr and FIRST, read RankGPT's sliding-window code, and compare with the reader's two-order probability design; distil a listwise teacher into a small model (the RankZephyr and jina-reranker-v3 recipes) — [rank_llm](https://github.com/castorini/rank_llm); [RankGPT](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/RankGPT); [jina-reranker-v3](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-reranker-v3).
5. **Link analysis and spam.** Compute PageRank and harmonic centrality on Common Crawl's host graph with cc-webgraph/WebGraph; read why CC prefers harmonic centrality; study TrustRank-style seed propagation and quality classifiers as features — [cc-webgraph](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-webgraph); [CC webgraph statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-webgraph-statistics/).
